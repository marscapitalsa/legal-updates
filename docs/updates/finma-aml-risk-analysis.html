FINMA Guidance 04/2026 · High relevance

AML risk analysis must drive the control framework

FINMA expects the AML risk analysis to express the institution’s actual risk tolerance and to determine the organisation, resources and day-to-day controls applied to its business.

What FINMA found

  • Some FinIA institutions already apply the methodology of Guidance 05/2023 by analogy; the required detail may be proportionate to lower risk.
  • Weaknesses remained in explicit exclusions of countries, client segments, services or products and in their alignment with the business model.
  • Methodological principles were not always applied correctly.

Why it matters

  • A generic risk assessment is unlikely to explain why the firm accepts particular PEP, country, product or intermediary exposures.
  • Risk appetite should be translated into measurable indicators and escalation thresholds.
  • The analysis should inform staffing, monitoring intensity, onboarding approvals and periodic review frequency.
Suggested follow-up: reconcile the AML risk analysis with current client/product data, document exclusions and quantitative tolerances, and minute management approval of the residual risk and resources.
