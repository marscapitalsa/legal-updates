Federal Supreme Court · Lenz & Staehelin analysis

Execution-only retrocessions: the actual relationship controls the result

What changed: in judgments 4A_501/2025 and 4A_503/2025, the Federal Supreme Court applied its execution-only retrocession case law to dual-currency deposits, including in-house products. It found no repayment duty on the facts because no relevant conflict of interest existed. [S1] [S2] [S3]

The judgments prescribe no standard IAM evidence package.

What the decisions establish

  • A true execution-only relationship involves execution of precise client orders; the contractual label is not decisive if advice or recommendations were actually provided. [S1] [S3]
  • The client's representative's expertise may be attributed to the client when assessing whether the transaction was genuinely execution only. [S1] [S3]
  • Repayment under the agency-law reasoning considered by the Court depended on a concrete conflict capable of influencing the intermediary. On these facts, the clients chose the product parameters and knew group entities would be counterparties, so the Court found no relevant conflict. [S1] [S2]
  • The in-house nature of the dual-currency deposits did not create a conflict on the specific facts; it is not a general exemption for all in-house products. [S3]

Correction to the earlier article

The judgments do not order a remuneration map, client-file checklist, waiver review, AMC approval process or transaction-by-transaction evidence package. Those operational recommendations have been removed. Product governance for an AMC is addressed separately under FINMA Guidance 03/2026 and must not be attributed to these decisions.
