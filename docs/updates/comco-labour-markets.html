COMCO draft communication · Consultation

Competition law applies to wage coordination and labour-market agreements

What changed: COMCO opened consultation on a draft communication explaining how Swiss competition law applies to wage agreements, no-poach arrangements and exchanges of employment information. Consultation closes on 30 September 2026; COMCO plans a final communication by year-end. [S1] [S2]

Source-based status

Applicability
Employment Competition
Relevant if the IAM exchanges employment information with other employers or is party to wage, no-poach or no-hire arrangements. [S2]
Accountable function
To be set internally if the condition applies.
Due / trigger
30 September 2026 only if submitting a consultation response. The source sets no IAM compliance implementation deadline; existing competition law already applies. [S1]
Status
Conditional review
  • If the condition applies, compare the arrangement or exchange with the risk factors in the draft. [S2]
  • If the IAM wants to comment, submit its response by 30 September 2026. [S1]
Source-supported evidence
The publication prescribes no particular IAM evidence package.

Risk factors identified by COMCO

  • Agreements on wages or other remuneration and no-poach or no-hire arrangements may restrict competition. [S2]
  • Current or future firm-specific salary ranges, bonuses and recruitment plans are competitively sensitive. Aggregated, anonymised and sufficiently historical information is generally less risky. [S2]
  • For benchmarking, the draft identifies risk-reducing factors such as an independent third party, aggregation, anonymity, historical data and participation by several firms; it gives five firms as an indicative benchmark, not a safe harbour. [S2]
  • No individual factor guarantees legality, and COMCO decisions remain based on the circumstances of each case. [S1] [S2]

What is not in the source

The draft does not itself prescribe meeting agendas, mandatory training, an inventory template, contract-review evidence or a particular approval process. Those have been removed as purported requirements. A legal review may still be appropriate if the IAM actually participates in the described conduct.
