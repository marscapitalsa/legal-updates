Business introducers need defined boundaries and documented oversight

SAM’s checklist and model agreement offer a practical control framework for third parties who introduce prospective discretionary-management or advisory clients.

Control questions

  • Is the introducer limited to introductions, or might its conduct amount to advice, distribution or another regulated activity?
  • Are remuneration, invoicing, conflicts, disclosure and termination rights explicit?
  • Do AML responsibilities, client communications, data protection, confidentiality and record retention have clear owners?
  • Have the introducer’s location, target clients and conduct been checked for cross-border restrictions?

Why it matters

  • The wealth manager remains responsible for its own client acceptance and conduct obligations.
  • Informal referral arrangements can create undisclosed conflicts, privacy breaches and misleading client expectations.
  • Periodic review should test actual behaviour, not only the signed agreement.
Suggested follow-up: inventory all introducers, risk-rate them, compare agreements and files against the checklist, and establish annual attestation plus incident/complaint escalation.
