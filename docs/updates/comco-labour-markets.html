COMCO consultation · Medium relevance · Deadline 30 September 2026

Competition law applies to employer coordination

What changed: COMCO opened consultation on guidance addressing wage-information exchanges and labour-market agreements after identifying detailed exchanges among more than 200 companies. What the IAM should do: if it participates in salary benchmarking or uses no-poach or non-solicitation arrangements, review the information exchanged, counterparties, aggregation and legal basis before continuing.

What matters

  • The consultation runs until 30 September 2026; COMCO expects a consolidated note by year-end.
  • Potentially problematic conduct includes agreements or coordination on wages, benefits, hiring, no-poach/non-solicitation and exchanges of competitively sensitive employment data.
  • Risk depends on content, recency, granularity, identifiability, market coverage, safeguards and whether the exchange supports a legitimate framework.

Why it matters and what may need to be done

  • Inventory compensation surveys, association working groups, recruiter arrangements, peer calls and bilateral HR exchanges involving competing employers.
  • Do not exchange current or future firm-specific salary bands, bonuses, hiring intentions, vacancies, benefit changes or retention strategy directly with competitors. Historical, aggregated data managed by an independent third party is generally less risky but still requires design review.
  • Review no-poach, non-solicitation and hiring restrictions in introducer, outsourcing, service-provider, transaction and settlement agreements. A restriction should have a documented legitimate purpose and proportionate scope; standalone market allocation is high risk.
  • Require an agenda for industry meetings, competition-law reminder, minutes and immediate objection/exit if discussion moves to sensitive employment terms. Do not remain silently in an improper exchange.
  • Give HR, executive management and association representatives practical competition training and an escalation contact before joining benchmarking exercises.
  • If the IAM wishes to comment on the draft, submit a reasoned response by 30 September 2026; otherwise track the final note and reassess controls once published.
Practical evidence: exchange inventory; survey methodology; legal assessment; meeting agendas/minutes; contract review; training attendance; escalation record.
