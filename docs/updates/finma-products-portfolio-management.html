FINMA Guidance 03/2026 · Supervisory expectations

Products in individual portfolio management: the controls FINMA actually identifies

What changed: after supervisory cases involving serious client losses, FINMA set out expectations on suitability, conflicts, product due diligence and risk control when portfolio managers use foreign funds, structured products—particularly AMCs—own or related products, products lacking equivalent supervision, and complex or illiquid instruments. [S1] [S2, sections 1–4]

Relevant where the IAM uses AMCs, foreign funds, illiquid products or the other product categories identified by FINMA. [S2, sections 1 and 4]

No new calendar deadline stated. Internal gap-review date: to be set. The duties described by FINMA apply when providing portfolio management and when selecting and monitoring affected products. [S2, sections 2–4]

Review required
  • Check client suitability and portfolio-level risk for each affected allocation. [S2, section 2]
  • Document the market offering and objective selection criteria; address and disclose conflicts. [S2, section 3]
  • Apply risk-based product due diligence and monitor the specific warning signs identified by FINMA. [S2, section 4]
  • Confirm that in-house or outsourced risk and compliance cover concentration, liquidity, valuation and conflicts. [S2, section 4]
Evidence
Suitability record; documented selection criteria and conflict treatment; product due-diligence evidence; portfolio/product risk monitoring; outsourcing responsibilities and access to information. [S2, sections 2–4]

FINMA's concrete expectations

  • Suitability: for retail clients, financial circumstances, investment objectives, knowledge and experience must be considered. The instrument must fit the agreed risk profile and investment strategy, and risks must be assessed in the context of the complete portfolio. [S2, section 2]
  • Product range: clients must be informed whether the market offering consists of own instruments, third-party instruments or both. Where both are offered, appropriate organisational measures are needed to avoid conflicts. [S2, section 3]
  • Objective selection: the selection process must use objective industry-standard criteria. Remuneration must not create incentives to favour own products; unavoidable conflicts must be disclosed. [S2, section 3]
  • Risk management: the risk framework must cover portfolio and product risks, including concentration, liquidity, valuation and conflicts. [S2, section 4]
  • Enhanced attention: FINMA identifies missing audited financial information, qualified audit opinions, a change or termination of auditor, and unreliable information on structure, valuation or liquidity as warning signs requiring risk-based due diligence. [S2, section 4]
  • Outsourcing: responsibility remains with the IAM. The institution must have sufficient resources and expertise to monitor the provider; responsibilities must be clear, the provider must receive complete information and the mandate must clearly cover portfolio and product risks. [S2, section 4]

Application to an AMC

Because FINMA expressly singles out AMCs among structured products, an AMC used by the IAM should be assessed under the four questions above: suitability within each client's portfolio; whether the offering and selection criteria are transparent and objective; whether IAM or related-party remuneration creates a conflict; and whether the issuer, structure, valuation and liquidity information supports risk-based due diligence and ongoing control. [S2, sections 1–4]

The Guidance does not prescribe an “AMC approval committee”, a named approver, a fixed allocation cap, a particular stress test, fixed monitoring intervals or a separate Armenian-bank placement procedure. If the IAM adopts any of those controls, they should be documented as internal risk decisions rather than attributed to Guidance 03/2026.

Application to foreign funds and illiquid assets

The review is to determine whether reliable audited, structural, valuation and liquidity information exists, whether the investment remains suitable at portfolio level, and whether concentration, liquidity and valuation risks are actually captured by risk management. FINMA does not specify a particular redemption threshold, valuation method or numerical concentration limit in this Guidance. [S2, sections 2 and 4]
