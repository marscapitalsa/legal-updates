Federal Supreme Court / Lenz & Staehelin · High relevance

Execution-only retrocessions: the label is not enough

In judgments 4A_501/2025 and 4A_503/2025 of 17 June 2026, the Swiss Federal Supreme Court confirmed that retrocessions received in a genuine execution-only relationship are not automatically repayable under Article 400 CO where the remuneration creates no concrete conflict of interest. The result also covered in-house structured products—but remains fact-specific.

What the Court decided

  • The decisions confirm the Court’s January 2026 ruling 4A_149/2025: execution-only retrocessions are not subject to restitution merely because they were received in connection with the transaction.
  • The controlling question is whether, under the actual contractual duties and circumstances, the remuneration could create a conflict capable of influencing the intermediary.
  • A relationship cannot be treated as conflict-free solely because it is labelled “execution only”; the circumstances require an overall assessment.
  • In the cases before the Court, the clients’ representatives gave precise orders for dual-currency deposits and knew that group entities would be counterparties. The Court found no relevant conflict and no repayment duty.
  • The judgment did not create a general exemption for discretionary portfolio management, investment advice, product recommendations or all in-house products.

Why it matters and what may need to be done

  • Classify by substance: review whether the IAM selects, recommends, compares, structures, promotes or merely executes the product. Any investment discretion or recommendation moves the facts away from the Court’s narrow execution-only analysis.
  • Map every remuneration flow: identify payer, recipient, calculation basis, product/transaction link, whether the amount depends on volume or product choice, and whether the IAM, bank or related party can influence the client’s decision.
  • For discretionary mandates: do not use the judgment to stop restitution or rely on an execution-only clause. Existing contractual, Article 400 CO, FinSA conflict/disclosure and valid-waiver analysis remains necessary.
  • For advice or solicitation: record whether a product was presented, compared or recommended; how the client selected it; and whether remuneration could bias that activity. A formally client-signed order does not erase preceding advice.
  • For in-house structured products and AMCs: product approval should separately document issuer/structurer, the IAM’s role, fee stack, target market, objective selection, alternatives, suitability, liquidity and conflicts. The Court’s restitution conclusion does not displace FINMA Guidance 03/2026.
  • Client documentation: align agreements, fee schedules, conflict disclosures, product documents, order records and waiver language with the actual service. Avoid generic clauses that combine execution, advice and management without clear boundaries.
  • File evidence: where execution-only treatment is relied upon, retain the precise unsolicited client instruction, product parameters chosen by the client, absence of recommendation/discretion, remuneration analysis and conflict conclusion.
Decision rule: no automatic restitution in a genuine execution-only case without a concrete conflict does not mean “retrocessions may always be retained.” The service and conflict must be assessed transaction by transaction and against the wider mandate.
