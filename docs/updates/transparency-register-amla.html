Federal Council · High relevance · Effective 1 October 2026

Swiss transparency register: two separate roles for an IAM

What changed: the Federal Council set 1 October 2026 as the start date for Switzerland’s transparency register and revised AMLA framework. What the IAM should do: prepare its own beneficial-owner reporting as a Swiss legal entity and, separately, define how Compliance will access the register for KYC and handle discrepancies as an AMLA financial intermediary.

What matters

  • LETA, revised AMLA and implementing ordinances enter into force on 1 October 2026; that date starts the statutory transition periods for affected entities.
  • The Federal Office of Justice operates the central register; an FDF control office checks completeness, accuracy and currency.
  • The register covers individuals controlling Swiss legal entities and specified foreign entities with a Swiss connection. It is not public.
  • Low- and medium-volume authorised users can access it through EasyGov; higher-volume users may use an API. EasyGov preparation is already available.
  • Financial intermediaries may consult the register and report discrepancies, but the register does not replace risk-based identification and verification of beneficial ownership.

Why it matters and what may need to be done

  • For the IAM itself: determine which natural person or persons ultimately control the legal entity under LETA, collect the prescribed identity and control-basis data, reconcile them with the share register and corporate documents, obtain missing information, and assign a person authorised to submit and maintain the filing through EasyGov. The board should validate the ownership conclusion where control is not obvious.
  • For changes: establish an internal trigger from company secretarial/share-transfer processes to the register owner. A change in shareholder, voting arrangement or other control mechanism must not wait for the annual AML review.
  • For client onboarding and review: update AML procedures to state when the register is queried, who performs the query, what evidence is retained and how the result is compared with declarations, corporate records and independent sources.
  • For discrepancies: define a case workflow: confirm the mismatch, seek clarification and supporting evidence from the client, reassess risk and beneficial ownership, decide whether a discrepancy report is required, and preserve the decision trail. Register access is through EasyGov or the API; discrepancy reporting is available to authorised financial intermediaries.
  • For access governance: register the organisation on EasyGov, nominate users, apply least-privilege access and logging, and decide whether expected query volume justifies API integration.
  • For timing: 1 October 2026 is the commencement date, not necessarily the filing deadline for every entity. Apply the transition period that corresponds to the entity category and formation date; new entities and later changes may follow shorter operational deadlines than existing entities.
Practical evidence: beneficial-owner determination memo; supporting corporate documents; board or authorised-person validation; submission receipt; change log; EasyGov access list; client-query evidence; discrepancy case file.
