Priority 1

Action required

These developments require a documented implementation step, control change or exposure review. Open the topic page for the detailed action plan.

Action required
FINMA · 04 Jun

AML risk analysis must function as a real management control

What changed: FINMA found that many institutions still do not connect their AML risk analysis to approved risk tolerance, measurable limits and evidence that controls work.

Deadline: 31 August 2026 · thereafter annually and upon material change

What the IAM should do: review the AML risk analysis and internal AML documentation; translate risk appetite into measurable limits, escalation triggers and resource allocation, and obtain documented approval.

Action required
Federal Council · 12 Jun

Transparency register and revised AMLA start on 1 October 2026

What changed: the Federal Council set 1 October 2026 as the commencement date for the new beneficial-owner register and revised AMLA framework.

Deadline: 30 September 2026 · operational readiness before commencement

What the IAM should do: identify the entity's beneficial owners, prepare the reporting data and assign responsibility for filings, KYC use of the register and discrepancy handling before the new regime starts.

Action required
SAM / SECO · Jul

SECO search results can no longer be printed or saved as before

What changed: SECO’s sanctions search no longer provides the former print/PDF output, so the old method of retaining a search result is unavailable.

Deadline: 7 August 2026 · evidence required for every screening event immediately

What the IAM should do: amend onboarding and periodic-screening procedures so every SECO search produces dated, retrievable evidence, such as a screenshot or equivalent tamper-evident record showing the query and result.

Action required
SAM · 08 Jul

Business introducers require a controlled onboarding framework

What changed: SAM and PwC issued a practical checklist for onboarding and controlling introducer relationships.

Deadline: 31 August 2026 · new introducers must be cleared before appointment

What the IAM should do: this applies expressly where the IAM pays a referral fee, commission, retainer, success fee, revenue share or non-cash benefit. Inventory active introducers and document due diligence, permitted role, remuneration, client communications, AML allocation, cross-border restrictions, monitoring and termination controls.

Action required
EU / UK / US · Jul

Russia sanctions require a four-regime exposure review

What changed: the EU adopted its 21st package, while the UK and US made additional Russia-related designation, cyber and licensing changes.

Deadline: 7 August 2026 · potential matches must be resolved before execution

What the IAM should do: screen relevant clients, counterparties, banks, instruments and payment routes, identify Swiss and foreign-law nexus, record the assessment and escalate matches or uncertain exposure.

Priority 2

Potentially relevant

No immediate implementation is required unless the stated condition applies. The condition is shown first so readers can decide whether to open the analysis.

Potentially relevant
FINMA · 03 Jun

FINMA sharpens expectations for products used in individual portfolio management

What changed: FINMA set out concrete supervisory expectations after cases involving unsuitable, illiquid, concentrated, proprietary and insufficiently reviewed products.

Relevant if: the IAM launches, issues, selects or manages an AMC or another complex, illiquid, concentrated or proprietary product. In that case product approval, enhanced due diligence, liquidity testing, conflicts controls and ongoing monitoring must be designed before use.

Potentially relevant
SIF · Jul

Swiss–US FATCA Model 1 transition moves further out

What changed: SIF now says reciprocal FATCA Model 1 can enter into force no earlier than 1 January 2029.

Relevant if: the IAM maintains FATCA procedures or supports an entity affected by the future Model 1 transition. Continue current Model 2 processes; no operational change is required before implementation details are fixed.

Potentially relevant
FINMA · 09 Jul

FINMA asks institutions to prepare for quantum-era cryptographic risk

What changed: FINMA published expectations for identifying cryptographic exposure and planning migration to quantum-safe solutions.

Relevant if: the IAM stores long-lived confidential data, operates material cryptographic systems or relies on IT providers whose migration planning must be challenged. This is a roadmap and vendor-governance issue, not an immediate technology replacement.

Potentially relevant
COMCO · 23 Jul

Competition-law scrutiny reaches labour-market agreements

What changed: COMCO opened consultation on guidance for wage-information exchanges and labour-market agreements after identifying detailed exchanges among more than 200 companies.

Relevant if: the IAM exchanges non-public salary information, participates in compensation benchmarking or uses no-poach or non-solicitation arrangements with other employers.

Read the conditional analysis →
Potentially relevant
Federal Supreme Court · 17 Jun

Execution-only retrocessions: the Supreme Court confirms a conflict-based test

What changed: the Federal Supreme Court held that retrocessions in a genuine execution-only relationship are not automatically repayable where no concrete conflict exists.

Relevant if: the IAM provides genuine execution-only services, receives retrocessions or uses in-house structured products. The judgment should not be extrapolated to discretionary management, advice or product promotion.

Watchlist — no immediate IAM action

  • FINMA liquidity ordinance: effective 1 January 2027 for banks and securities firms; not directly applicable to a conventional independent portfolio manager, but relevant to regulated counterparties.
  • Swiss implementation of the EU 21st sanctions package: monitor SECO and Federal Council decisions; do not represent EU measures as already incorporated into Swiss law before publication and entry into force.
  • Federal Supreme Court / Gazprombank: the April judgments reinforce substantive verification of beneficial ownership where PEP, offshore and other red flags remain unresolved. This is reflected in the AML workstream above.
