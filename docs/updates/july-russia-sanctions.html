EU / UK / US · Foreign-regime updates

July Russia sanctions: new EU and UK measures and targeted US licences

What changed: the EU adopted a 21st sanctions package on 23 July, the UK announced Russia-related cyber designations on 13 July, and OFAC issued targeted Russia-related general-licence updates on 8 and 24 July. These publications do not by themselves amend Swiss sanctions law. [S1] [S2] [S3] [S4]

Verified July developments

  • EU, 23 July: the Council says the 21st package added 48 individuals and 170 entities and targeted Russian energy, financial services and crypto, among other sectors. [S1]
  • UK, 13 July: the UK announced coordinated sanctions against 24 persons and entities associated with Russian state-linked cyber activity and cybercrime. [S2]
  • US, 8 July: OFAC issued Russia-related General License 13R. [S3]
  • US, 24 July: OFAC issued General License 131H concerning specified transactions involving Lukoil International GmbH and updated FAQs 1224 and 1225. [S4]
  • Correction: OFAC's 20 and 27 July list maintenance was previously described as new Russian designations, including a new industrial entity. The cited OFAC notices do not support that statement, so it has been removed. [S5] [S6]

Swiss-law limitation

No specific July SECO or Federal Council publication implementing the EU's 21st package is cited here. The foreign measures are therefore presented as foreign-regime developments, not as changes already incorporated into Swiss law. Absence of a cited Swiss publication is not presented as proof that no later Swiss action occurred; current SECO measures must be checked when making a legal decision.

What was removed from the earlier article

The package announcements do not require every Swiss IAM to conduct a “four-regime exposure sweep”, set screening events, block transactions, create a nexus memorandum or apply foreign ownership-and-control tests. Those actions depend on applicable law, contractual provider requirements and the actual facts. They may be prudent where a nexus exists, but are not represented as direct requirements from these publications.
