Sanctions screening needs a defensible evidence trail

The loss of SECO’s former print/PDF function is operationally small but control-significant: the IAM must still prove what was searched, when, by whom and with what result.

What matters

  • According to SAM’s July Newsflash, SECO confirmed that direct printing or saving of search results is no longer available in its previous form.
  • While SECO assesses replacement functionality, dated and time-stamped screenshots are an interim evidence method.
  • SECO’s search is a supporting tool, not a complete sanctions or ownership-and-control assessment.

Why it matters and what may need to be done

  • Define screening events: before client acceptance, before relevant payments or transfers, at periodic review, when ownership or connected persons change, and promptly after material list updates for exposed relationships.
  • Retain the exact query string, spelling variants and transliterations, selected filters, result screen, date/time, source/list version where available, operator and reviewer. A screenshot filename alone is not enough unless it links unambiguously to the client and screening event.
  • Screen the client, beneficial owner, authorised persons, settlor/trustee/protector/beneficiaries where relevant, connected entities, banks and transaction counterparties. Assess entities owned or controlled by listed persons even if the entity name itself does not match.
  • Define false-positive clearance criteria and require second-level review for plausible matches. A potential match should block onboarding or execution until resolved; a confirmed match requires immediate escalation under the applicable sanctions and AML procedures.
  • Store evidence in the controlled client or transaction file with retention, access and data-protection safeguards. Sample-test that the captured screen is legible and reconstructs the decision.
  • If screening is outsourced or automated, document source coverage, update frequency, fuzzy-matching settings, alert ownership, service failures and the IAM’s independent oversight.
Practical evidence: screening procedure; event matrix; screenshots/search reports; match-resolution note; reviewer sign-off; update log; provider controls; periodic sample-test results.
