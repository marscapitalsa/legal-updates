FINMA Guidance 05/2026 · Medium relevance

Quantum risk is now an operational-resilience planning issue

FINMA does not demand an immediate wholesale replacement of encryption. It expects institutions to understand their exposure, prioritise long-lived sensitive data and establish a credible migration path with providers.

What matters

  • Perform an institution-specific quantum risk analysis and create a cryptographic inventory.
  • Address “harvest now, decrypt later” exposure for information that must remain confidential for many years.
  • Develop a roadmap for quantum-safe encryption and crypto-agility.
  • Include outsourced IT, cloud, communications and software providers in the assessment and transition.

Why it matters and what may need to be done

  • Assign an accountable technology/operational-risk owner and involve information security, data protection, business continuity and outsourcing oversight. The governing body should receive a proportionate risk conclusion and material dependencies.
  • Inventory where cryptography protects client portals, email, file transfer, laptops, databases, backups, digital signatures, remote access and provider-to-provider connections. Record algorithm/protocol, key management, vendor, data class, retention horizon and replacement dependency.
  • Prioritise KYC, identity, legal, transaction and portfolio data whose confidentiality must survive for years. Consider whether data intercepted today could create material client or firm harm if decrypted later.
  • Ask critical providers for their cryptographic inventory, post-quantum roadmap, supported standards, test plan, dependencies and contract cooperation. “We follow industry standards” is not a sufficiently testable answer.
  • Build crypto-agility into procurements and changes: ability to replace algorithms and certificates without redesigning the whole service, software or data format.
  • Set risk-based milestones for discovery, remediation design, testing and cutover; track unsupported legacy systems and compensating controls. Reassess when recognised standards or vendor capabilities change.
Practical evidence: quantum risk assessment; cryptographic/data inventory; provider responses; prioritised roadmap; architecture decisions; test results; residual-risk approvals.
