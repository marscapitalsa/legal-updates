FINMA Guidance 04/2026 · Supervisory expectations

AML risk analysis: risk tolerance, limits and control effectiveness

What changed: FINMA explains how supervised institutions should connect their AML risk analysis with business strategy, risk tolerance, quantitative limits, exceptions, controls and the resulting net risk. For FinIA institutions, the expectations apply by analogy and proportionately to their actual exposure. [S1] [S2, sections 1–2]

What FINMA specifically requires or expects

  • Deliberate tolerance: risk tolerance should include conscious exclusions. FINMA examples include no foreign PEPs, no complex structures, no business from specified countries or industries, and no crypto or trade-finance business. These are examples, not mandatory exclusions for every IAM. [S2, section 2.1]
  • Exceptions: exceptions to policy should require executive-level approval, be centrally recorded, monitored by number, volume, trend and quality, and reported regularly to the governing body. They cannot be used as a general or permanent way around limits. [S2, section 2.2]
  • Meaningful indicators: not every value needs to be a limit. FINMA examples include the number and AuM of elevated-risk relationships and PEPs, number of exceptions, and exposure to high-risk countries outside the target market. Multiple figures, particularly client count and AuM, should be used. [S2, section 2.3]
  • Complete criteria: client segments, domicile or residence, products and services must be included even where assessed as low or medium risk. Country and sector detail for a FinIA institution is proportionate to its business; categories may be grouped where no increased risk exists. [S2, sections 2.3–2.4]
  • Controls and net risk: controls must be described specifically or referenced through a precise control inventory. Effectiveness should be supported by meaningful information such as sample results. Aggregate net risk must be compared with approved tolerance. [S2, sections 2.4–2.5]

What this means for Russia-linked clients

The Guidance does not classify Russian nationality or a Russian nexus as automatically high risk and does not prescribe a Russia-specific percentage. Because it expects geographic criteria, country exposure and business outside the target market to be represented, the IAM should determine—in its own documented methodology—how the actual residence, domicile and country exposure of its client base enter the analysis. [S2, sections 2.3–2.4]

FINMA's only numerical illustration in the Guidance is a hypothetical medium-tolerance institution that does not focus on PEP business and sets a limit of up to 15% PEP relationships. That is an example of converting tolerance into a measurable figure; it is not a safe harbour and cannot be reused as a Russia limit. [S2, section 2.3]
